# Morgan Stanley 2026 Global Economic Outlook: Summary Notes

**Source:** [2026 Economic Outlook: Moderate Growth With a Range of Possibilities](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/global-economic-outlook-2026)
**Publisher:** Morgan Stanley Research
**Published:** November 19, 2025
**Featured economist:** Seth Carpenter, Chief Global Economist

> These are summary notes written in my own words, not a copy of the article. For the full text, charts and disclosures, please read the original at the link above.

---

## Big Picture

Morgan Stanley expects global growth to ease slightly and then hold steady, with inflation cooling across most regions. That leaves room for several central banks to lower rates. Resilient U.S. household spending and AI-related business investment are the main supports. The range of possible outcomes is unusually wide.

## Headline Forecasts

| Area | Forecast |
|---|---|
| Global GDP (Q4/Q4) | ~3.0% in 2025; 3.2% in 2026 and 2027 |
| U.S. real GDP | 1.8% in 2026; 2.0% in 2027 |
| China real GDP | 5.0% in 2026; 4.5% in 2027 |
| Euro area real GDP | 1.1% in 2026; 1.3% in 2027 |

## Regional Growth Notes

- **U.S.:** A noticeable slowdown is expected in the first half of 2026, followed by a pickup in the second half. Drivers include consumer and business spending and easier monetary and fiscal policy. Faster AI adoption could lift productivity over time.
- **China:** Government support is front-loaded, so growth is stronger in 2026 and fades in 2027 as stimulus effects wear off.
- **Euro area:** Growth stays modest. German fiscal support is partly offset by budget tightening in France and Italy.

## Inflation Outlook

- **U.S.:** Core PCE is expected to tick up in early 2026 because of tariffs and immigration restrictions, then resume a gradual decline. It is forecast at 2.6% at the end of 2026 and 2.3% at the end of 2027.
- **Euro area:** Headline inflation is expected to fall short of the ECB's 2% target, at about 1.7% through the end of 2026 and in 2027.
- **Japan:** Inflation has recently run above target, but the underlying trend is softer. It is forecast to dip below 2% in late 2026 and return to 2% in 2027.
- **China:** Core CPI should stay positive, but the GDP deflator is expected to stay below zero as excess capacity clears slowly.

## Central Bank Expectations

| Central bank | Baseline expectation |
|---|---|
| **Federal Reserve** | Cuts through April, then a long pause with the target range at 3%–3.25%. A new Fed Chair arrives in Q2 2026, but the Fed's reaction function is expected to stay broadly the same. |
| **ECB** | Currently on hold, but two cuts are forecast in 2026, taking the policy rate to 1.5% by midyear. |
| **Bank of England** | Cuts to 2.75% in 2026, then a pause. |
| **Bank of Japan** | Hike to 0.75% in December 2025, hold through 2026, and possibly resume hikes in 2027 to 1.25%. |

## Alternative Scenarios

1. **Demand-driven U.S. upside:** Households and businesses, helped by government spending and tax changes, spend and invest more. U.S. real GDP could exceed 3% in 2026.
2. **Productivity-driven upside:** AI adoption speeds up and its economic effects arrive sooner than expected. Growth is stronger while prices stay subdued. Unemployment holds steady, with firms needing somewhat fewer workers but no broad job losses.
3. **Mild U.S. recession:** The lagged effects of monetary policy, tariffs and immigration restrictions hit harder than expected. Quarterly GDP growth could turn negative in the first half of 2026 and unemployment could rise. The Fed eases but does not cut to zero, since the downturn is mild. The slowdown could spill over to Europe, Japan and China.

## Key Takeaway

The outcome depends heavily on the U.S. consumer, given a slowing labor market, and on how quickly AI delivers real productivity gains. Carpenter sees the U.S. as the economy most likely to produce material upside for global growth.
